What to include in a Shopify chargeback evidence packet

A useful response does more than attach records. It connects the dispute reason to a short factual explanation, a readable timeline, and the strongest supporting evidence.

8 minute readUpdated September 10, 2026

The short version

Start with the exact dispute reason. Put the strongest proof first. Use a one-page summary, a chronological timeline, labeled exhibits, and only the records that help the reviewer evaluate that specific claim.

1. Match the packet to the dispute reason

Shopify groups disputes by reason, and the useful evidence changes with the claim. A delivery scan can matter for a “product not received” case, while an “unrecognized payment” case needs records that connect the cardholder or account to the transaction. Read the reason and submission deadline before collecting files.

Shopify says merchants generally have a limited evidence window and that the customer's bank—not Shopify—decides the outcome.

2. Lead with a one-page response summary

The summary should identify the merchant, order, disputed amount, processor reason, response deadline, and the merchant's factual position. Keep it neutral. Do not make accusations or bury the reviewer in an essay.

Example structure

“The merchant fulfilled order DEMO-78421 to the destination supplied at checkout. Carrier records show delivery on September 6. The customer contacted the merchant on September 8, and the merchant responded with the delivery record and offered to open a carrier trace. Exhibits A-F document the transaction, shipment, delivery, and resolution attempt.”

3. Build a chronological timeline

A timeline lets the reviewer understand the order without reconstructing it from filenames. Include only dates that advance the case:

  1. Order placedOrder reference, items, amount, and customer-entered information
  2. Payment authorizedRelevant authorization or verification results shown by the platform
  3. Order fulfilledFulfillment record, shipment date, carrier, and tracking number
  4. Delivery or access recordedDelivery scan, signature, pickup record, login, download, or service usage
  5. Customer contactedThe customer's report and the merchant's response or resolution attempt
  6. Dispute receivedReason, amount, case reference, and response deadline

4. Include reason-specific evidence

Product not received

  • Order confirmation
  • Fulfillment record
  • Carrier tracking history
  • Delivery confirmation or signature
  • Customer communication
  • Merchant resolution attempt
  • Shipping policy

Payment not recognized

  • Order and account history
  • Billing and shipping match
  • AVS or CVV results shown by the processor
  • Login, IP, or device record when lawfully available
  • Prior undisputed orders
  • Customer acknowledgement

Product not as described

  • Product page as presented at purchase
  • Order confirmation and selected variant
  • Product photos or quality records
  • Customer complaint
  • Refund, return, or replacement offer
  • Applicable return policy

Duplicate charge

  • Transaction IDs and timestamps
  • Invoices or receipts
  • Explanation of separate orders
  • Void or refund record
  • Processor ledger showing final settlement

5. Label every exhibit

Use a simple exhibit index: exhibit letter, document name, and why it matters. Remove duplicates, unreadable screenshots, and unrelated personal or payment information. Make sure every statement in the summary can be traced to a source record.

ExhibitDocumentPurpose
AOrder confirmationIdentifies the transaction and purchased items.
BFulfillment recordShows when the merchant released the order.
CDelivery confirmationDocuments the carrier's delivery event.
DCustomer email threadShows the issue and merchant response.

6. Check the packet before submission

  • Every date and statement matches the source record.
  • The response directly addresses the processor's reason.
  • The strongest evidence appears before supporting detail.
  • Screenshots are readable at normal zoom.
  • Unrelated personal and payment information is removed.
  • The final file meets the processor's format and size limits.
  • The response is submitted before the displayed deadline.
  • A submission confirmation is retained.
